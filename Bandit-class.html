What the numbers mean

RL (Rescorla–Wagner / Q-learning)
Keeps a running value for each machine: Q ← Q + α·(reward − Q). The prediction error (reward − Q) drives learning. α is the learning rate: high α means the last few outcomes dominate. β is the inverse temperature of the softmax choice rule: low β means noisy, exploratory choices; high β means reliably picking the higher-valued machine. 2 parameters.
RL + perseveration
The same learner plus a pull toward repeating the previous choice, regardless of outcome: logit += κ·(1 if this was the last choice). κ > 0 is “sticky” choice; κ < 0 is a tendency to alternate. 3 parameters. Following Lau & Glimcher (2005).
Bayes
An ideal observer that treats each machine as a coin with an unknown payout rate. It starts with a flat Beta(1,1) belief, counts wins and losses, and values each machine by its posterior mean. It assumes payouts never change, so after the reversal it switches only once the new evidence outweighs everything it saw before. Only β is free. 1 parameter.
Bayes + novelty
The Bayesian observer plus a bonus for the machine it knows least about: value = posterior mean + η·posterior SD. η > 0 means directed exploration toward the less-sampled option; η < 0 means sticking with the familiar one. 2 parameters. This is the uncertainty-bonus form used by Frank et al. (2009) and Badre et al. (2012).
Best model and ΔBIC
Each model is fit to each student by maximum likelihood (grid search) and scored with BIC = −2·log-likelihood + k·ln(trials). The lowest BIC wins. The k·ln(trials) penalty keeps richer models from winning just by being more flexible. ΔBIC is the gap to the runner-up: under 2 is weak evidence, over 6 is strong. “Chance-level” means a 0-parameter coin flip scores better than every model. Fits start after 10 trials.
Caution with 30 trials
With 30 choices, simulations show the fitting reliably separates the RL family from the Bayes family, but often can’t tell a model from its extended version. Read individual κ and η values as rough, and lean on class-level patterns. This is a good point to raise with students: model and parameter recovery depend on how much data you have (Wilson & Collins, 2019).
